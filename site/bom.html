9Perfboard + Pi on the back cover; contact plate + M5 contacts outside, 4 × M3×16CoverRing lugs on the M5 nuts inside.
9bDock: sprung rods (collar, spring, M3 washer epoxied over the back), wires soldered to the rod ends, up the column to the controller board; wings on with 4 × M3×12; 4 rubber feet; stand it against a wallDock partsRods slide freely and spring back, ~4 mm travel. Polarity: the rod that meets the robot's + pad (robot Y −25, its right-hand side; the LEFT rod when you look at the dock face) = +25.2 V.
9cDock controller: build the board per the site's 'Wiring diagram > Dock' page, flash firmware/dock_beacon onto the ATtiny85 (8 MHz internal); LEDs into the mast holes (4 sides + 1 in the centre tube), board standing in the mast's hollow, rod wires (+ = LEFT rod looking at the face) and jack wires down the column. Bench test first (page's checklist): rods 0 V with nothing on them, 25.2 V with 112 kΩ across them, off on a short. Stick the 2 reflective strips into the face guides. Robot: 4 TSOP38238 in their hoods on the top skin, leads to J8Dock parts, ISP programmer, 112 kΩ + 1 Ω/5 W test resistorsPhone camera sees the LEDs flicker. Robot telemetry 'ir' shows bits for the receivers facing the dock; walk round it: they change.
10Front panel: AI Camera (focus set to ~1.5 m first) + polycarbonate window, sonar, eyes + diffusers + retainers, headlight discs + retainers, headlight driver; 4 rubber bumpers on the face; USB-C socket in the left side wallFront panel + left wallHeadlights: 1 A fuse in the PDB lead; Pi pin 32 -> driver PWM.
11Lidar on top; power button (front-right); switch board inside the back cover, above the Pi; arm button
12Power-up checks: 5 V rail, CAN ~60 Ω, battery divider calibrationMultimeter
13Charging checks BEFORE connecting the battery: dock rods 25.2 V (+ on the rod that meets the robot's + pad); robot leaned on the dock by hand: 25.2 V on the M5 nuts inside; IP2368 end voltage 25.2 V; pads read 0 V undockedMultimeterThen a supervised first charge in the LiPo bag.
13bDocking: press 'Dock & charge' with the robot anywhere within ~4 m of the dock. It turns until it hears the beacon or sees the tape, homes in, lines up 45 cm out on the dock's axis and reverses in, leans back: CHARGING (blue LED). 'Undock' stands it up and drives it 0.4 m outWeb appThe first dock saves the dock's map position; afterwards 'dock' plans a path there from anywhere on the map (and on low battery by itself). The 'ir' bits in telemetry show which receivers hear the beacon.
14Flash ESP32, set up Pi; wheels-off test, balance, then stand-upEverythingRe-simulate with real masses first.
