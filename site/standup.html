Three phases, one handover

Both wheels are driven hard and the reaction torque whips the body up (LIFT). The moment the body is within 0.35 rad (20°) of vertical the balance controller takes over (CATCH) and must absorb whatever rotation it inherits. A trial counts only if the body then stays inside 12° for the final second. The LIFT phase, and only that, is what the two controllers do differently — the balancing code is identical and untouched by the training.

Why the learned one wins

None of this was designed in. The policy was given a score to maximise and found these three behaviours by itself; they were measured afterwards.

1 · It never trips the safety cut-off

The hand-written push is a fixed 3.20 N·m held for as long as it takes. That spins the wheels past 114 rad/s — a guard meant to catch a runaway robot — so the motors are killed while the body is still rising. A good attempt is thrown away and the robot starts over. The policy modulates its torque and stays under the limit.

Peak wheel speed  hand 117.6 · model 104.6 rad/s Cut-off at 114.0 rad/s

2 · It arrives gently

Both swing up about as hard, peaking near 11–12 rad/s of rotation. The difference is the last 150 ms: the policy brakes into the handover, so the balance controller inherits a body that is almost still. The hand-tuned one is still turning twice as fast when it lets go.

Rotation at the catch  hand 3.66 · model 1.77 rad/s Median over 60 robots

3 · It gets up first time

Because nothing aborts the push, the policy needs one attempt instead of two or three. That makes it both faster and far more repeatable: its slowest stand-ups still finish in 0.80 s, where the hand-tuned one can take twice that.

Every number here is measured. The replays are recorded through ml/evaluate.py's own control loop, so they are the same runs the scores come from; tilt is the simulator's true body angle rather than the robot's own estimate. Physics runs at 1 ms, the controller at 500 Hz, and the learned policy decides every 10 ms.

Scores are 30 randomised robots × 2 starting sides over three independent sets (180 trials). Each robot has its own mass, centre of mass, tyre grip, battery voltage, motor strength and sensor bias. The learned policy is in the repository but switched off (standup_mode = 0): it has only ever run in simulation.
