Balancing robot V1.1 — dock wiring

The charging dock's electronics: charger input, a rod switch that only makes the rods live when the robot is on them, and the IR beacon. One ATtiny85 runs both (firmware/dock_beacon). Dock parts in the BOM, rows "Dock controller board", "Dock IR beacon LEDs" and "Dock contact rods".

Safety

  • Only safety extra-low voltage in the dock: 25.2 V DC from a certified, short-circuit-protected wall charger. Nothing in the dock touches mains.
  • The rods are dead unless the robot is on them. The board feeds a weak sense current (≤ 0.25 mA) and only switches the 25 V on when it sees the robot's own 112 kΩ detect divider for 0.5 s. Nothing on the rods, a coin, a key, a wet hand (a few kΩ) or a short keeps them off.
  • While charging it switches off for 30 ms every 2 s and checks the robot is still there: the rods go dead within 2 s of the robot leaving (the robot ignores dropouts under 0.15 s).
  • Short or overload while live (rod voltage under 15 V): off within ~10 ms, 5 s pause, then back to sensing. Three faults within a minute latch it off until the dock is unplugged (status LED blinks fast).
  • Q2 is off without firmware: its gate is pulled to its source, Q3 is held off by a 100 k pull-down. A blank or crashed ATtiny leaves the rods dead.
  • F1 (1.1 A PTC) limits any fault current the charger's own protection doesn't; D1 clamps plug-in spikes; Q1 blocks a wrong-polarity supply (the robot would otherwise see −25 V on its sense pin).
  • Robot side: its pads are dead when off the dock (SB560 diode) and fused at 5 A; the BMS protects each cell; charging at 1 A is 0.7 C for the 1400 mAh pack.
  • First charges supervised, robot on a non-flammable surface; LiPo bag for storage. The IR LEDs are low-power (≈ 10 mA average) 940 nm: invisible, harmless at normal distances.

LED current: (5 V − 2 × 1.4 V) / 22 Ω ≈ 100 mA per all-round string, (5 V − 1.45 V) / 33 Ω ≈ 105 mA for the centre LED, in short bursts (≈ 10 mA average). Q2 at 1 A dissipates 0.07 W: no heatsink. Sense thresholds are in firmware/dock_beacon/dock_logic.h and are unit-tested (test_dock_logic.cpp).

Bench test before the first dock

  1. Before fitting the ATtiny: plug the charger in. V25 = 25.2 V, 5 V rail = 5.0 V, rods 0 V (Q2 off). Reverse a bench supply into J1: nothing on V25 (Q1 blocks).
  2. Flash dock_beacon.ino, fit the ATtiny. Rods open: rod+ reads ~18 V on a meter (the sense feed through 100 k; it collapses under any meter load below ~1 MΩ, so use a 10 MΩ DMM). Status LED blips every 2 s.
  3. Put 112 kΩ (100 k + 12 k) across the rods: after 0.5 s they go to 25.2 V and the status LED stays on. Remove it: 0 V within 2 s.
  4. Short the rods with a 1 Ω / 5 W resistor (not a wire): rods off at once, LED blinks 2 Hz, then retries; after 3 shorts it blinks fast and stays off until unplugged.
  5. Phone camera at the mast: the IR LEDs flicker purple. Robot telemetry 'ir' shows bits for the receivers facing the dock.
  6. First real dock: the robot leans on, the status LED goes on after 0.5 s, the robot shows CHARGING (blue).
